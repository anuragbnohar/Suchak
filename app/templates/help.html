Getting started

What the app is for, and the shape of an ordinary day on it.

What Drishti does

It collects what is being said publicly about the entities you supervise, and puts it in front of you worst first.

News coverage, social media and consumer forums are gathered per entity and assessed one item at a time: which risk areas it touches, how serious it is, whether it reports a customer grievance, and whether it needs an action. Nothing is judged against rules hard-coded by a programmer — every item is judged against your team's own written definitions of risk area and severity, which live on the Policy screen and which your team can edit.

Your rulings are the point of the system, not a formality. Every review is kept, and the next time something comparable arrives it is shown back to you as precedent, with the action you took last time already suggested.

Nothing is collected until someone presses Fetch on the Entities screen, unless an automatic sweep has been switched on. So an empty screen on a fresh install means nothing has been fetched yet — not that nothing is happening.

A working day

If you do nothing else, do this.

  1. {% if sees.dos %}Queue → To review{% else %}RD View → Queue{% endif %}. Work down the list. It is already sorted worst first, so the top of the page is where your attention is worth most.
  2. Open each item and record a review. Read the story, correct the classifier where it is wrong, and say whether anything needs doing. This is the one action the whole app is built around.
  3. Tick Actionable where a follow-up is owed, assign it to someone and give it a date. It appears on the To-do page until it is closed.
    4. {% if sees.dos %}
  4. Check To-do for what you and your team owe. The badge beside the tab counts what is still open.
    5. {% endif %}
  5. Use {% if sees.dos %}Dashboard{% if sees.rd %}, RD View{% endif %}{% elif sees.rd %}RD View{% endif %} or Complaints when the question is the shape of the week rather than any one story — what is rising, where, and against whom.

Controls on every screen

Learn these once and they behave the same everywhere.

  • Every number is a link. If a figure is on the screen, clicking it opens exactly the items it counts — no more and no fewer. That is a rule the app holds itself to, so you can always ask a total to show its working.
  • Date. The period a screen covers. The default is All dates, deliberately: a fraud case four months old is still what belongs in front of a supervisor. Narrow it when the question is "what came in this week". Two figures ignore it on purpose — Awaiting review and Open actions are all-time, because an item unread for five weeks is exactly what you must not lose.
  • Entity. Which entity the screen is about. Your choice follows you across the tabs, so moving from Dashboard to Queue keeps the same bank rather than snapping back to the first on the roster.
  • Filter chips. When filters are in force, a row above the content spells them out. Each has an ✕ to drop just that one, and there is a clear filters link to drop them all. You are never stuck in a filter you cannot see.
  • The build number in the footer says which version you are running. It is the way to check an update actually landed.

The screens

In the order they appear in the bar at the top of the window.

{% if sees.overview %}

Overview

Three ways to read the same period, across every entity at once.

The tabs change the question, not the data:

  • All regulated entities — who needs attention. One row per entity, sorted by high-severity volume.
  • By severity — how serious the record is.
  • By risk area — what kind of problem is showing up.

The two category views span every entity, and each row keeps its per-entity split as clickable chips beside it — because the Queue is per entity, and a cross-entity total nobody could open would break the rule that every figure leads somewhere. Totals open a cross-entity queue that labels each row with the entity it belongs to.

Items still awaiting classification are excluded here and counted in a footnote. They carry no verdict yet, and counting them would file every one of them under low.

{% endif %} {% if sees.dos %}

DoS View → Dashboard

One entity at a time: what its record looks like right now.

  • The tiles — total items, the three severities, complaints, awaiting review, and open actions with an overdue sub-count. Each opens the items behind it.
  • Items by risk area — where this entity's exposure sits.
  • Daily volume — the shape of the last few weeks. Click a bar to open that single day.
  • Recent high-severity items — the short list you would read first if you read nothing else.
  • Alert matches — which of your named alerts this entity tripped, counted for news and for social posts side by side. Each figure opens the list of items behind it.
  • Complaints by topic — grievances for this entity, by kind.
  • Linked organizations — other bodies the coverage named alongside this entity.

DoS View → Queue

The working list: everything collected, waiting for your ruling.

Items are sorted worst first — severity, then how actionable, then how recent — so working from the top down is the right order. Official and trusted sources rank ahead of the rest within the same severity. Coverage of one event by five outlets is grouped into a single row, so a resignation reported everywhere does not fill the page five times.

The tabs are the item's state:

  • To review — not yet ruled on. This is your list.
  • Reviewed / Dismissed — what you have ruled on, kept so the trail survives.
  • Filtered out — screened out before reaching you as not supervisory-relevant. Worth a glance now and then to check the screen is not dropping things it should keep.
  • Rejected — the story was not about this entity at all (a different bank with a similar name, usually).

The Reviewed box against each item marks it reviewed — or, unticked, sends it back for another look — without opening it. The tick is remembered until somebody changes it, and it never locks anything: the item stays open to a full review — severity, categories, notes — whatever the box says, and unticking never erases a review already recorded.

The filters narrow the list by risk area, severity, trusted sources only, a named alert, a linked organization, or complaints only. Clicking a figure on the Dashboard or Overview brings you here with the matching filter already set.

Social-media grievances are not listed here. They are a workstream of their own with their own screen — fifty forum complaints would otherwise bury the day's news.

Reviewing an item

The one action the app is built around. Open any item from any list to get here.

The page shows the story, every source that carried it, what the classifier concluded and why, and — where there are any — similar items you have already ruled on, with the action you took then. Then you record your own view:

  • Is this relevant? No — dismiss takes it off the queue. Use it when the item is not about this entity or not supervisory at all.
  • Risk areas — tick every area the item touches. Your ticks override the classifier's everywhere the app counts risk areas.
  • Complaint topics — what the customer is aggrieved about. Leave all unticked if it is not a grievance; that is itself a ruling, and it removes the item from the Complaints screen.
  • Severity — high, medium or low, against your team's written criteria on the Policy screen.
  • Actionable? Yes puts it on the To-do page until somebody closes it. Assign an owner and, if it matters, a due date.
  • Action taken / proposed — from sought clarification through to referred for enforcement review. Where you have handled something comparable before, the same action arrives already selected.
  • Notes — anything the next person needs to know.

Wherever you correct the classifier, your correction wins — on every screen, not just this one. Reviews are never overwritten by a later fetch, and the history of who changed what is kept on the item.

{% if sees.dos %}

DoS View → Social media

What customers are complaining about, in their own words.

Posts from Reddit, consumercomplaints.in and, where it is switched on, X — grouped by what the complaint is about. Deliberately narrower than the Queue: a post is listed only where a grievance was found in it. Praise, questions and noise are counted in the footer but not shown, because the question this screen answers is what are customers complaining about, not what was said.

  • The topic tabs narrow to one kind of grievance; the source tabs to one platform. A post that trips one of your named alerts carries a solid red ⚑ chip, and the Dashboard's alert panel counts social matches beside the news ones.
  • Set aside — posts a reviewer marked as no use for pattern-finding: pure venting, nothing specific named, already resolved. They stay readable here but are kept out of Insights and Complaints.
  • Auto-excluded — posts the app learned to skip from what reviewers set aside before. Check it occasionally to confirm it is not learning the wrong lesson.
  • Posts and complainants — the line under the list counts both. One customer writing five times is five posts but one aggrieved customer, and only the second number answers "how many people". Where a source names nobody, that post counts as its own complainant, so the figure can overstate the number of people and never understate it. The complainant is recorded as a post arrives, so posts collected before the app started doing that carry none: until some do, the screens say not yet recorded rather than showing a number that would just be the post count again.
  • One entry per person — a customer who writes six times about the same failed refund is one aggrieved customer, and six rows of it would read as six complaints. Their repeats fold into one entry, marked 6 posts from this person or a thread of 6 posts, with the rest a click away in the order they were posted. Nothing is discarded: every post keeps its severity, topics and review, every count on the page is taken before the folding, and Show every post hands back the plain list. Only a named poster is ever folded — a post naming nobody stands on its own, so the entry count can overstate how many people are behind a set of complaints and never understate it. The same person writing about two banks is two complaints, not a repeat; and where several customers reply under one bank's post they stay several entries, because sharing a conversation is not the same as being the same person. Set the others aside as duplicates, inside the fold, is the ruling that follows: it keeps the first post counted and takes the repeats out of Insights, and any of them can be restored.
  • The Reviewed box on each post works exactly as it does on the Queue: tick to mark the post looked-at, untick to send it back — it keeps its state across sessions, never locks the post's full review form, and teaches the classifier nothing. Only inputs given on the review form itself (severity, categories, action, comments) count as a ruling.

DoS View → Insights

The pattern across many complaints, rather than any one of them.

Which product or process keeps drawing grievances, each finding backed by the complaints it was drawn from and carrying a proposed line of enquiry. Anyone on the team may read them. {% if sees.insights_generate %}Generating a fresh set costs a model call, so the Generate button is limited to team leads and the super admin — you have it.{% else %}Generating a fresh set costs a model call, so that stays with team leads and the super admin.{% endif %}

A finding needs a minimum number of complaints behind it before it will be drawn at all. Three angry posts are not a pattern, and the screen says so rather than inventing one.

DoS View → To-do

Everything a review said somebody would do.

An item lands here when a reviewer ticked Actionable. The tabs are Open, Done and All; the filters narrow to what is yours, what is overdue, or a severity or risk area. The count beside the tab in the bar above is the number still open.

  • Closing. You may close a follow-up that is yours; a team lead may close any on their entity.
  • Reassigning. A lead may hand work to anyone on the entity. A member may move a task sideways — to a fellow member, or one sitting with nobody — but not take one off a lead's or the super admin's desk.
  • Reopening. A closed follow-up can be reopened; nothing here is destroyed.
{% endif %} {% if sees.rd %}

RD View

A region rather than an entity: what is happening in one RBI office's territory.

The regional overview has two tabs, and the difference between them matters:

  • Headquartered here — the entities whose head office falls in this region. This is the beat proper.
  • In-region news · other entities — coverage that names a district or state in this region but concerns an entity headquartered elsewhere. A national bank's branch trouble in your state shows up here, and nowhere else.

The Queue and Social media tabs beneath are the same screens described above, scoped to the office rather than to one entity.

{% endif %}

Complaints

Every customer grievance you can see, counted together — news coverage and social media in one place.

A workspace of its own rather than a screen inside another, because it spans every entity you can see and both kinds of source. It opens on no individual complaint at all: the question is the shape.

  • Complaints and Complainants — the first counts grievances, the second the people behind them, and the gap between the two is how much of the volume is one customer repeating. It counts social posts only: a news article names a publication, not an aggrieved customer, so including news would force the two tiles to agree and hide the very thing they are there to show. Until posts start arriving with a complainant recorded, the tile reads not yet recorded rather than a number it cannot stand behind.
  • Three heat matrices — grievance type against the entity, against the type of entity, and against the state. All three are shaded on one scale, so equally dark means equally busy wherever you look. Every cell opens the complaints it counts.
  • The filters — date, type of entity, entity, location, source and severity — narrow every figure on the page.
  • Reading the numbers. A row total counts complaints; a cell counts complaints carrying that category. Most grievances carry more than one, so a row of cells legitimately adds up to more than its total. The page says so under every matrix rather than quietly reconciling them.
  • Why a figure never reads zero wrongly. Each control counts as though its own choice were not made. With Medium selected, the High severity tile still shows how many high-severity complaints there are to switch to. The tiles for the total, the entities and the categories — and the list at the foot — show the selection itself.
  • Where a complaint happened is read from the story: the classifier's own reading first, then the districts and states the text names, with the entity's own name excluded so Bank of Maharashtra is never filed under Maharashtra for that reason alone. A national story and one that names no place each get an honest row of their own rather than being dropped.

Two things are left out on purpose: social posts a reviewer set aside as no use for pattern-finding, and anything a reviewer dismissed.

Setting the app up

The screens that decide what is collected and how it is judged.

Alerts

Named things your team wants flagged the moment they appear.

An alert is a rule in your own words — a name, and the conditions that raise it. Every item collected is judged against the active ones, and a match shows as a solid red ⚑ chip on the item: on queue rows, on social cards, and on the item's own page. It is the one chip meant to be seen before anything else on the row.

  • Alerts in force — the list, with the conditions of each, who it covers, and whether it is switched on. {% if sees.alerts %}You can add one and switch any on or off.{% else %}Team leads and the super admin add and toggle them.{% endif %}
  • Scope — an alert can cover one entity, every entity of a type (every NBFC, every Urban Cooperative Bank), or every entity there is. A type-scoped alert covers entities of that type added later too, without anyone having to remember. Scopes beyond a single entity are the super admin's to set, because they fire on other teams' entities.
  • Matches — what each alert has actually caught, news and social counted separately, each figure opening the list behind it. An alert reading none yet is either new, worded too narrowly, or genuinely quiet — worth knowing either way.
  • Re-check stored items against alerts — alerts are judged when an item first arrives, so one added today shows nothing on older items until the super admin presses this. It walks everything already collected, one model call per item, in the background. While it runs, its progress shows under the button and a second press is refused; the page keeps the last run's outcome afterwards, so a missed pop-up loses nothing. The Policy screen's complaint Re-score button behaves the same way.

An alert changes nothing about how an item is judged — not its severity, not its risk areas. It is a flag for your attention, and the Dashboard counts what each one has caught.

Policy

The written standards every item is judged against. Change these and the next classification changes with them.

  • Risk area definitions — what your team means by each of the seven areas.
  • Severity criteria — two scales, side by side. One for events at the institution (a default, a breach, a run); a second for customer grievances, judged by what the complaint alleges rather than how loudly it is written. An item carrying complaint topics is scored by the grievance scale; everything else by the institutional one. Every severity on every screen is judged against these texts.
  • Negative list — subjects not to analyse at all.
  • Trusted sources — the outlets whose reporting ranks ahead of the rest within the same severity, and which the Queue's source filter shows. Editing the list re-tiers every stored item immediately.

The four policy texts are editable by the super admin. You can read them here — worth doing, because they are the standard your own reviews are measured against.

Per entity, the buttons run separately: news, social (Reddit, forums, YouTube comments — free) and X (paid, billed per post read, which is why it never runs with the others). The picker beside each sets how far back that fetch looks. Re-fetching is incremental — it only classifies what it has not seen before.

  • Fetch. Collection is run by team leads and the super admin from this screen. The Last fetch column tells you when each entity was last collected, and whether anything failed.

  • Aliases matter more than anything else here. Attribution is an exact phrase match, so a headline is kept only if it contains one of an entity's aliases. The legal name alone rarely appears in print — add the short forms a journalist would actually write. A missing alias is the usual reason an entity looks quiet when it is not.
  • News languages — a regional entity is covered in the local press first. A language only helps if the entity also has an alias written in that script.
  • X handles — every handle complaints are sent to, the care handle and the main one alike.

{% if sees.settings %}

Settings

Collection windows, budgets and caps. Super admin only.

How far back a fetch looks by default, how many items a source may return, how often the automatic sweep runs (off unless you switch it on), and the spend limits on the paid sources. A value that no longer sits at its default is marked, so a knob turned six weeks ago is visible rather than forgotten.

The People section below the knobs is the roster: who may sign in and what they may do. You can add a colleague, set a password for somebody who has forgotten theirs, and remove an account that should no longer have access — which ends the session it is holding at that moment, while keeping the person's name on the reviews they recorded. Those rulings are supervisory record and are never rewritten. You cannot remove your own account, nor the last super admin.

An account can also be marked a guest. They take part in the review work exactly as their role allows — reviewing, setting aside, closing to-dos, raising an alert — but what is kept back is everything that spends money or reshapes the installation. They cannot fetch, nor press Generate insights, Re-check alerts or Re-score, each of which sets the model running over what is already stored. They cannot open Settings or Policy, which is what the classifier judges by. They cannot add, rename or remove people, nor add, edit or delete an entity or its aliases. Those controls are hidden from them and refused if reached another way. It can be lifted or re-applied at any time, taking effect on their next click rather than their next sign-in.

Add a second super admin before relying on this application. There is no reset link: one administrator who forgets a password is a locked-out system.

Reference

The vocabulary, and the limits of your own sign-in.

Your account

Reached from Account beside your name, top right.

Change your own password here. Your current one is asked for, so a session left open on a shared desk cannot be used to lock you out of your own account. A password needs at least eight characters.

Forgotten it? This prototype sends no email, so there is no reset link. A super admin sets a new one for you from the Settings screen — which is also why there should always be two super admins.

The words the app uses

Risk area
One of seven supervisory areas — Credit, Market, Liquidity, Operational, Governance, Cybersecurity, and Conduct & Consumer Protection. An item may touch several.
Severity
High, medium or low, judged against your team's written criteria rather than anything fixed in the software.
Complaint topic
What a customer is aggrieved about — mis-selling, recovery practices, service disruption, unauthorized transactions, charges & fees, harassment, account access / KYC, or other. No topics means the item is not a grievance.
Actionable
A reviewer decided somebody must do something. It creates the follow-up on the To-do page.
Dismissed
A reviewer ruled the item not relevant. It leaves the queue but is kept.
Filtered out
Screened out before reaching a reviewer as not supervisory-relevant. It has its own tab so the screening can itself be checked.
Rejected
The story turned out not to be about this entity — usually a different organisation with a similar name.
Set aside
A social post that is a real grievance but no use for pattern-finding. Readable, but kept out of Insights and Complaints.
Alert
A named thing your team wants flagged, with the conditions that raise it. A match shows as a red ⚑ chip on the item. Called a factor in earlier versions of this app.
Trusted source
An outlet on your team's own list. Its reporting ranks ahead of the rest within the same severity and carries a ✓.
Alias
A way the press writes an entity's name. Aliases decide what a fetch keeps.
Precedent
A review you recorded earlier on a comparable item, shown back to you while you review this one — and shown to the classifier as it judges it. Only a review recorded on the form counts: ticking the Reviewed box, on the Queue or the Social media tab alike, says you have looked at an item, not what you concluded, so it teaches the classifier nothing either way.
